DGCA RTR(A) Exam Pattern & Syllabus (Updated per CAR Sec 7 Series G Part VI)

The Restricted Radio Telephone Operator's Certificate of Proficiency (RTR-A) is issued under the Wireless Planning and Coordination (WPC) Wing of the Ministry of Communications in coordination with the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA), India.

Pursuant to the latest DGCA Civil Aviation Requirements (CAR) Section 7 Series G Part VI, the examination and training framework is structured into three core syllabus domains and two examination phases.

Regulatory Framework & Guidelines

All RTR(A) training and examination standards in India comply with DGCA CAR Section 7 Series G Part VI, ICAO Annex 10 (Vol I & II), ICAO Doc 9432 (Manual of Radiotelephony), and ITU Radio Regulations.

Syllabus Breakdown (Annexure A)

I. Regulations and Procedure

Covers national and international telecommunication rules, aviation regulations, and standard flight notification procedures:

  • International Telecommunication Convention & Radio Regulations (ITU).
  • ICAO Annex 10 (Vol I & II): Aeronautical 'Q' Code signals, abbreviations, and standard procedures.
  • General Radiotelephone Communication Procedures for distress, urgency, and direction finding.
  • Maritime Mobile Service distress communication procedures.
  • ICAO Spelling Alphabet & Figures: Phonetic transmission rules.
  • Licensing & Aircraft Radio Equipment: Minimum required radio equipment under ICAO Annex 6 and DGCA CAR in India.
  • Indian Flight Information Regions (FIRs): Major radio communication, navigation facilities, and frequency allocations.
  • Meteorological Codes & Pre-Flight Services: METAR, TAF, SPECI, and pre-flight briefing.
  • Notices to Airmen (NOTAMs): Structure and usage issued by Indian Civil Aviation Authorities.

II. Radio Principles and Practice

Covers the physical and technical fundamentals of aeronautical radio communications:

  • Electrical Fundamentals: Volts, Amperes, Ohms, Watts, Wavelength, and Frequency calculations.
  • Radio Frequency Propagation: Day vs. night propagation, skip distance, fading, ground shadow, and HF frequency selection for maximum efficiency.
  • Air-Ground Communication Systems: Aircraft intercoms, cabin announcements, and long-range HF/VHF comms.
  • Radio Navigation Aids & Hardware: Operation of Microphones, Headphones, Squelch, Automatic Volume Control (AVC), Volume tuning, Simplex/Duplex operations, and interference mitigation.

III. Radio Telephony (Practical Phraseology)

Covers practical phase-of-flight transmissions using standard ICAO phraseology:

  • General Operating Procedures: Transmitting techniques, call signs, time/number formats, read-back requirements, and confirmation rules.
  • Aerodrome Control (Surface & Circuit): Engine start, push-back, taxi, takeoff, circuit join, final approach, landing, go-around, and essential aerodrome information.
  • General ATS Surveillance Service: Radar identification, vectoring, radar assistance, and alerting phraseology.
  • Approach & Area Control: IFR/VFR departures and arrivals, airway crossings, en-route holding, Automatic Dependent Surveillance (ADS), and Oceanic control procedures.
  • Distress & Urgency Procedures: Transmitting MAYDAY / PAN PAN, imposing/terminating radio silence, emergency descents, and total radio communication failure procedures.
  • Special Flight Handling: SELCAL, fuel dumping, wake turbulence, wind shear, Direction Finding (DF), and ACAS/TCAS conflict resolution.

Exam Structure & Evaluation

Phase 1: Practical Transmission

Simulates real-world flight scenarios. Candidates are given a flight plan and unexpected inflight situations (emergencies, weather diversions, system failures) and must execute correct radio transmissions under strict time limits.

  • Total Marks: 100
  • Passing Marks: 50%
  • Focus: ICAO phraseology, brevity, read-back accuracy, and handling MAYDAY/PAN PAN.

Phase 2: Viva Voce (Oral Interview)

Oral interview conducted jointly by WPC and DGCA examiners evaluating technical radio principles, regulations, navigation aids, and practical scenario handling.

  • Total Marks: 100
  • Passing Marks: 50%
  • Focus: Radio propagation, VOR/ILS/NDB/GPS, ITU/DGCA regulations, aircraft radio apparatus.

Recommended Training Curriculum (Annexure B)

DGCA CAR Section 7 Series G Part VI mandates a minimum 48-Hour Training Module for approved Radiotelephony Training Organizations (RTTO):

Module Subject Area Recommended Hours
Module 1 Regulations (Aeronautical Telecom & Nav Aids) 5.0 Hours
Module 2 Radio Principles & Practice 10.0 Hours
Module 3 Procedures & Radiotelephony (incl. 2 Full Mock Tests) 33.0 Hours
Total Program Comprehensive RTR(A) Preparation 48.0 Hours

Capt. Pahil's Exam Strategy

In Phase 1, if you make an error during a transmission, do not freeze or start over. Immediately say "CORRECTION", state the correct item, and proceed. Examiners grade on clarity, standard phraseology, and calm execution under pressure.
