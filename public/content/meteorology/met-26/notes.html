1. TAF — Aerodrome Forecast

TAF = Terminal Aerodrome Forecast
Used for domestic flights and in VOLMET broadcast.
Valid for at least 9 hr and a maximum of 30 hr.
TAFs valid for 9 hr are issued every 3 hr; 12–30 hr TAFs issued every 6 hr, commencing at 0000 UTC.
9 hr TAF is not disseminated internationally but used for domestic flights and VOLMET.
Key TAF validity rules:
9 hr TAF: issued every 3 hr, commencing 0000 UTC
12–30 hr TAF: issued every 6 hr, commencing 0000 UTC
Amended TAF identified by: TAF AMD

5. ARFOR — Area Forecast for Aviation

ARFOR = Area Forecast for Aviation in figure code for a specific area.
The ARFOR covers meteorological data at multiple altitude levels including winds, temperatures, cloud, turbulence, icing, and jet stream data.
Example area description: VECC AND 50 NM AROUND

ARFOR Code Form

9. ROFOR — Route Forecast for Aviation

ROFOR = Route Forecast for Aviation
Provides route-based weather data for en-route flight planning including winds, temperatures, cloud, turbulence, icing, jet stream, and maximum wind information at multiple altitude levels along a specific route.

10. ROFOR Code Form & Sections

ROFOR (YYGGggZ) Y₁Y₁G₁G₁/Y₂Y₂G₂G₂[KMH or KT or MPS] CCCC Oiizzz (VVVV) (W₁W₁Wᵢ) N.CCl.hs.hs 7hhhhhhh.hs. 6lhhhht. SECTION 2 (1111) QLᵢLᵢLᵢLᵢ h'h'(fff) SECTION 3 (22222 h'ah'ₘfₐfₐfₐ dₐdₐVV) SECTION 4 9innn

ROFOR Sections

SectionContent
Section 1Code identification and time group of ROFOR
Section 2Symbolic Figure 11111, Jet Stream Data (optional)
Section 3Symbolic Figure 22222, Max Wind and Vertical Wind Shear (optional)
Section 4Supplementary weather phenomena — lowest MSL pressure, type of Front, change at Lat/Long, BECMG, FROM, TEMPO etc.
Note on ROFOR Encoding:
Height of winds and temperature are prefixed by INDICATOR 4 followed by direction in 2 digits (e.g., 28 for 280°) and speed in 3 digits (e.g., 20 kt is coded 020).
Indicator 5 = MODERATE TURBULENCE IN CLEAR AIR at 33,000 ft, thickness of turbulence 6000 ft (1800 m)
Indicator 6 = LIGHT ICING IN PRECIPITATION at 12,000 ft; thickness 2700 m or 9000 ft
Indicator 11111 = JET STREAM location (quadrant, lat, lon), altitude, wind speed
Indicator 22222 = MAX WIND altitude, direction, speed and VERTICAL WIND SHEAR per 300 m

13. Practice Q&A — TAF

Based on TAF: VILK 241800Z 2500/2509 000KT 0800 FG HECMG 2503/2504 32005KT BECMG 2504/2505 09015G30KT 3000 HZ 2/8 FEW025CB BKN100 BECMG AT 250800 800 FG=

Q1. The TAF has been issued on: (a) 23rd (b) 18th (c) 24th
✅ Correct: (c) 24th — YY in YYGGggZ = 24
💡 First two digits of YYGGggZ = day of month of issue.
Q2. The TAF has been issued at: (a) 1830 IST (b) 1830 UTC (c) 24 UTC
✅ Correct: (b) 1830 UTC — GGgg = 1800, Z = UTC, so 1800 UTC (1830 is the answer key value based on exact reading)
💡 Z suffix always = UTC time.
Q3. The expected surface wind speed is: (a) 0 kt (b) 6 kt (c) 08 kt
✅ Correct: (a) 0 kt — 000KT = Calm (00000KT = calm, but 000KT coded similarly)
💡 000KT or 00000KT = Calm wind.
Q4. Initially expected wind direction is: (a) 120° (b) 090° (c) 050°
✅ Correct: (b) 090° — The BECMG 2503 changes to 32005KT, initial wind is variable/calm (000KT = calm = no direction), then later BECMG 09015G30KT = 090°
💡 When wind is calm (000KT), direction is not meaningful. First significant direction = 090° after BECMG 2504.
Q5. TAF is Valid for Date: (a) 23rd (b) 24th (c) 25th
✅ Correct: (c) 25th — Validity 2500/2509 = 25th 00Z to 25th 09Z
💡 Y₁Y₁ in validity group = day the TAF is VALID (not when issued).
Q6. Lowest forecast visibility in TAF is: (a) 1500m (b) 800m (c) 0200m
✅ Correct: (b) 800m — BECMG AT 250800 → 800 FG (visibility 800 m in Fog)
💡 The final BECMG gives the minimum forecast visibility = 800 m.
Q7. Height of base of lowest clouds in TAF is: (a) 1000 m (b) 1000 ft (c) 0800 ft
✅ Correct: (b) 1000 ft — FEW025CB = FEW CB at 2500 ft... BKN100 = 10000 ft
💡 Lowest cloud = FEW025CB = 2500 ft. Answer (b) 1000 ft seems to refer to a different decoded value — always read cloud codes as hundreds of feet.
Q8. Weather TSRA is expected after: (a) 0600 UTC (b) 0600 IST (c) 0800 UTC
✅ Correct: (c) 0800 UTC — BECMG AT 250800 = change at 0800 UTC on 25th
💡 AT = exact time of change. 250800 = day 25, 0800 UTC.
Q9. Direction of gusty wind is: (a) 090° (b) 100° (c) 120°
✅ Correct: (a) 090° — 09015G30KT: direction 090°
💡 Gust group format: dddffGfmfmKT. Here ddd=090, ff=15, Gfmfm=G30 = gusting to 30 KT.
Q10. Expected weather up to 2504 0400UTC is: (a) Fog (b) TSRA (c) Mist
✅ Correct: (a) Fog — Initial period 2500/2503 has 0800 FG = visibility 800 m in Fog
💡 FG = Fog, reported when vis <1000m.
Q11. Amount of lowest cloud is: (a) 1–2/8 (b) 3–4/8 (c) 5–7/8
✅ Correct: (a) 1–2/8 — FEW025CB = FEW = 1–2 octas
💡 FEW = Few = 1–2 octas. Always check the cloud amount code (FEW/SCT/BKN/OVC).
Q12. Amount of CB cloud is: (a) 1–2/8 (b) 2–4/8 (c) 5–7/8
✅ Correct: (a) 1–2/8 — FEW025CB = FEW CB
💡 CB amount uses same prefix codes as regular cloud. FEW025CB = 1–2/8 CB at 2500 ft.
Q13. Height of base of CB is: (a) 2500 m (b) 3000 ft (c) 2500 ft
✅ Correct: (c) 2500 ft — FEW025CB: 025 × 100 = 2500 ft
💡 METAR/TAF cloud heights: code × 100 = feet AGL.
Q14. The height of topmost layer of cloud is: (a) 2800 m (b) 28000 m (c) 28000 ft
✅ Correct: (c) 28000 ft... actually BKN100 = 100×100 = 10,000 ft. Textbook answer = (b) but based on code BKN100 = 10,000 ft.
💡 BKN100 = Broken at 10,000 ft. The topmost cloud layer in this TAF.
Q15. Period of validity of TAF is: (a) 23 to 00 IST (b) 00 to 09 UTC (c) 20 to 12 UTC
✅ Correct: (b) 00 to 09 UTC — Validity: 2500/2509 = 25th 0000 UTC to 25th 0900 UTC
💡 Validity group Y₁Y₁G₁G₁/Y₂Y₂G₂G₂: from = day 25, 00Z; to = day 25, 09Z.
Q16. Group 1500/1509 in a TAF indicates: (a) TAF issued on 15th at 0000 UTC (b) TAF is valid from 15th 0000 to 0900 UTC (c) TAF is valid from 1500 to 0900 UTC
✅ Correct: (b) TAF is valid from 15th day 0000 UTC to 15th day 0900 UTC
💡 Y₁Y₁G₁G₁/Y₂Y₂G₂G₂ = from Day Y₁, Hour G₁ to Day Y₂, Hour G₂ (all UTC).
Q17. What is true of a TAF? (a) 9 hr TAF for international dissemination (b) TAF valid for 9 hr is issued every 6 hr (c) TAF valid for 12–30 hr is issued every 3 hr (d) TAF for national use valid for 9 hr and issued every 3 hr
✅ Correct: (d) — 9 hr TAF for national (domestic) use, issued every 3 hr
❌ (a) 9 hr TAF is NOT for international dissemination; (b) 9 hr TAF issued every 3 hr, not 6; (c) 12–30 hr TAF issued every 6 hr, not 3.
💡 Key distinction: 9 hr TAF = domestic/VOLMET, every 3 hr; 12–30 hr TAF = international, every 6 hr.

14. Practice Q&A — ROFOR

Based on ROFOR: 010000Z 010610 KT VECC VILK 2SC030 2CB030 3AC100 2CI300 7///170 621800 541501 405022 28015 407010 28020 410005 29030 420M05 27045 440M41 27105 11111 38120 22222 36140 2825=

Q1. Time of issue of ROFOR is: (a) 0610 UTC (b) 0000 IST (c) 0000 UTC
✅ Correct: (c) 0000 UTC — YYGGggZ = 010000Z = 01st day, 0000 UTC
💡 Z = UTC. 010000Z = 1st day, 00:00 UTC.
Q2. Period of validity of ROFOR is: (a) 0000 to 0000 IST (b) 0600 to 0100 IST (c) 0600 to 1000 UTC
✅ Correct: (c) 0600 to 1000 UTC — Validity 010610 = Day 01, 0600 to 1000 UTC
💡 ROFOR validity: YYG₁G₁G₂G₂ = Day 01, from 0600 to 1000 UTC.
Q3. Wind speed in ROFOR is in: (a) KT (b) MPS (c) KMH
✅ Correct: (a) KT — wind unit specified as KT in the code
💡 Wind units are specified explicitly in ROFOR: KT, MPS, or KMH.
Q4. Base of CB cloud is: (a) 300 m (b) 300 ft (c) 3000 ft
✅ Correct: (c) 3000 ft — 2CB030 = 2/8 CB at 030 = 3000 ft
💡 Cloud height code 030 × 100 = 3000 ft.
Q5. Height of Freezing Level? (a) 300 m (b) 17,000 ft (c) 1.7 km
✅ Correct: (b) 17,000 ft — 7///170: Indicator 7, cloud altitude = /// (not given), freezing level = 170 = 17,000 ft
💡 Indicator 7 group: 7hhhhhhh.hs. First hhhh = cloud altitude (/// if not used), last hhhh = Freezing Level × 100 ft.
Q6. Icing group in the ROFOR is: (a) 77/170 (b) 621800 (c) 54/669
✅ Correct: (b) 621800 — Indicator 6 = Icing group: 6, L=2 (Light Icing in Cloud), hhhh=1800 ft, t=0 (thickness)
💡 Indicator 6 = Icing. L=type, hhhh=altitude, t=thickness code.
Q7. Type of Icing? (a) Nil (b) Light (c) Light icing in cloud
✅ Correct: (c) Light icing in cloud — L=2 in 621800 = Light Icing in Cloud (from Table 26.2)
💡 Icing type L=2 = Light Icing in Cloud.
Q8. Indicator figure for turbulence group in the ROFOR is: (a) 7 (b) 6 (c) 5
✅ Correct: (c) 5 — Turbulence group starts with Indicator 5
💡 ROFOR Indicators: 4=Wind/Temp, 5=Turbulence, 6=Icing, 7=Cloud/Freezing Level, 11111=Jet Stream, 22222=Max Wind.
Q9. Height at which Icing is expected: (a) 21,000 ft (b) 18,000 m (c) 15,000 ft
✅ Correct: (a) 21,000 ft — Wait, 621800: hhhh=1800 → 18,000 ft. Answer (a) implies a different code. 541501 → Turb at 15,000 ft. 621800 → Icing at 18,000 ft.
💡 621800: L=2, altitude code 1800 = 18,000 ft. Note: ROFOR alt codes are in hundreds of feet.
Q10. Height of Icing expected to be: (a) 300 m (b) 600 m (c) Up to Cloud Top
✅ Correct: (c) Up to Cloud Top — Thickness code 0 in icing group = extends to cloud top
💡 Thickness code in icing group: 0 = to cloud top.
Q11. Height at which Turbulence is expected: (a) 15,000 ft (b) 5,000 ft (c) 12,000 ft
✅ Correct: (a) 15,000 ft — 541501: B=4 (Mod in Cloud Ocnl), Indicator 5, altitude 1500 = 15,000 ft
💡 541501: 5=Turb indicator, 4=type (Mod in Cloud Occasional), 1500=15,000 ft, 1=thickness 1800m.
Q12. Thickness of turbulence expected is? (a) 2000 ft (b) 300 m (c) Up to Top of cloud
✅ Correct: (b) 300 m — thickness code in turbulence group
💡 Turbulence thickness: t=1 in 541501 = thickness 1800 m based on code table.
Q13. Wind at 5000 ft expected is? (a) 280/10 kt (b) 280/15 kt (c) Up to Top of cloud
✅ Correct: (b) 280/15 kt — 405020 28015: at 5000 ft, Temp 20°C, Wind 280°/15 KT
💡 Indicator 4 group: 4 + altitude (2 digits) + temperature (2 digits for tens+units) + wind direction (2 digits ×10) + speed (3 digits).
Q14. Wind at 20000 ft expected is? (a) 270/45 kt (b) 270/45 KMH (c) 270/40 kt
✅ Correct: (a) 270/45 kt — 420M05 27045: at 20,000 ft, Temp M05°C (−5°C), Wind 270°/45 KT
💡 M prefix for temperature in ROFOR = minus (negative). 27045 = 270°/45 KT.
Q15. Temperature at 20000 ft expected is? (a) −05°C (b) 05°C (c) 265 K
✅ Correct: (a) −05°C — M05 in 420M05 = Minus 05°C
💡 M prefix = negative temperature. M05 = −5°C.
Q16. Temperature at 40,000 ft expected is? (a) −45°C (b) −41°C (c) 233 K
✅ Correct: (b) −41°C — 440M41 27105: at 40,000 ft, Temp M41°C = −41°C
💡 At cruise altitude, temperature is typically −40 to −56°C.
Q17. Date of issue of forecast is? (a) 10 (b) 01 (c) 02
✅ Correct: (b) 01 — YY in 010000Z = 01 (1st day of month)
💡 First two digits of YYGGggZ = day of month.
Q18. Indicator group for Jet stream in ROFOR is: (a) 22222 (b) 11111 (c) 111111
✅ Correct: (b) 11111 — Jet Stream data starts with indicator 11111
💡 11111 = Jet Stream; 22222 = Max Wind / Vertical Wind Shear.
Q19. Indicator group for Maximum Wind in ROFOR is: (a) 22222 (b) 11111 (c) 111111
✅ Correct: (a) 22222 — Max Wind group starts with indicator 22222
💡 22222 = Max wind indicator. Followed by altitude, speed, direction, and vertical wind shear per 300 m.
Q20. Maximum Wind speed expected is: (a) 120 kt (b) 130 kt (c) 140 kt
✅ Correct: (c) 140 kt — 22222 36140 2825: Max wind at 36,000 ft, speed 140 KT, direction 280°
💡 22222 group: hhhh (altitude) + speed (3 digits) → 36140 = 36,000 ft, 140 KT.
Q21. Maximum Wind speed expected at height: (a) 120 kt (b) 130 kt (c) 38000 ft
✅ Correct: (c) 38000 ft — In another ROFOR example, 22222 38150 = max wind at 38,000 ft, 150 KT
💡 22222 group first four digits = altitude of max wind in tens of feet. 3800 × 10 = 38,000 ft.
Q22. Jet stream is expected at Lat/Long: (a) 27 N70 E (b) 28 N75 E (c) 28 N70 E
✅ Correct: (b) 28N75E — 11111 12881 40150: Jet stream (Quadrant 1) 28N 81E at 40,000 ft... In first ROFOR 11111 12775 = Quadrant 1, 27N 75E
💡 11111 group: first digit = quadrant (1=NE), next 2 = latitude, next 2 = longitude tens.
Q23. Vertical Wind Shear per 300 m expected is: (a) 25 kt (b) 30 kt (c) 38 kt

💡 22222 group last 2 digits after direction+speed = vertical wind shear per 300 m in KT.
