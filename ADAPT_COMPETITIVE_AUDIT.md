*Research and comparison, 2026-08-10. Prepared for Capt. Pankaj Pahil.*

## 0. What I did, and the one thing I did not do

I read the publisher's own site product page by product page, ran a competitor's
free multitasking simulator end to end in a browser, walked the Indian
competitor's product, and read the coaching-industry write-ups against all of it.

**I did not register an account on the ADAPT platform or buy the £48–£245
practice bundles.** Creating accounts and entering payment details is outside
what I am permitted to do, and it is not a judgement call I get to make. If you
want the inside of the real thing — the actual FAST screen, the actual ball
game, the actual report PDF — that purchase has to be made by you or by a
student, and it is the single most valuable thing that could be added to this
research. £68 (Cadet Essentials) buys Maths, Physics, Cognitive Reasoning, FAST
and Ball Game with 5–15 attempts each over three months. **My recommendation: buy
that bundle.** Everything below is built from public first-party specification;
the bundle would convert it into observed fact.

Sourcing rule used throughout: **first-party (Symbiotics' own pages) is marked
as such and trusted. Coaching-company blogs are marked and treated as hearsay.**
Several widely repeated "facts" about this test come only from blogs and one of
them is flatly contradicted by the publisher (see §1.4).

## 1. The real ADAPT — what it actually is

### 1.1 Who makes it, who uses it

Made by **Symbiotics Ltd** (UK). Delivered on their own platform at
`adapt2.symbioticsltd.co.uk`. They state coverage of 170 locations across 90
countries.

Confirmed users relevant to an Indian student:

| Operator | Basis |
|---|---|
| **IndiGo** — cadet pilot programme, via the Skyborne training partnership (announced June 2024) | third-party, two independent trackers |
| **CAE** — cadet screening across multiple airline programmes | third-party |
| Wizz Air (personality component), Flightrule Aviation (FAST + APQ) | third-party |

One competitor states plainly that *"Indigo is the only airline as of 2026 which
takes the ADAPT test… Air India uses AON cut-e and COMPASS… Akasa takes a
personal interview and a sim check."* That is a competitor's claim, not a
verified fact, but it matches everything else I found and it is the right
framing for the Indian market: **ADAPT ≈ IndiGo cadet. cut-e/COMPASS ≈ most
others.**

### 1.2 The full catalogue (first-party, employer-facing)

Symbiotics sell **17** instruments. A cadet does not sit all of them.

Knowledge: Maths Progressive · Physics Progressive · Mechanical Reasoning ·
Aviation Knowledge (CPL+ only) · English Language · English Spoken Assessment.
Cognitive: Cognitive Reasoning · Logical Reasoning.
Aptitude/psychomotor: **FAST** · **Control & Co-Ordination (Ball Game)**.
Behavioural: **ADAPT Personality Questionnaire (APQ)** · Aspects Leadership ·
Crew Resource Management · Emotional Intelligence · Motivation Drivers ·
Preferences Under Pressure · Instructor Capabilities.

**What a cadet actually sits** — the six Symbiotics themselves package as the
cadet products: Maths, Physics, Cognitive Reasoning, FAST, Ball Game, APQ.

Knowledge practice tests use **a fixed question paper — the same one on all five
attempts** — deliberately, so their revision guidance can be acted on between
sittings.

### 1.4 Three things the coaching blogs get wrong

1. **"FAST needs a joystick."** Symbiotics' own FAST page: it "requires **no
   joystick**." Multiple Indian and UK coaching sites say otherwise. **This
   matters to you directly: a keyboard/mouse browser implementation of the
   multitasking test is not a compromise, it is faithful.** The joystick belongs
   to the Ball Game, not to FAST.
2. **"50 aviation-knowledge questions in an hour."** That is the *Aviation
   Knowledge* test, which Symbiotics scope to "CPL holders and above" — not part
   of cadet screening. One popular guide presents it as a cadet section.
3. **Publisher contradicts itself on tablets** — the FAST product page says "the
   test is tablet compatible", the bundle page says FAST and Co-Ordination
   "can not be taken on a tablet". Unresolved; assume laptop.

### 1.5 The four structural ideas that make ADAPT what it is

Strip away the branding and this is what the design actually does:

1. **Difficulty ramps inside a single sitting** — knowledge papers are
   "Progressive"; FAST escalates task count and intensity as it runs.
2. **Learning rate is itself the score.** FAST is run twice and the *improvement*
   is read as trainability. The Ball Game reports improvement across attempts
   against a comparison group. This is the deepest idea in the battery and the
   one most preparation products miss entirely.
3. **Sub-scores, not one number.** Cognitive Reasoning returns five figures, not
   one.
4. **Sten (1–10) plus a colour band** is the reporting currency for the
   psychomotor test — not stanine.

## 2. What I ran myself

### 2.1 Airmappr free FAST simulator (no sign-up) — ran it live

Three concurrent tasks for **12:00**, across **three rising-difficulty phases**:
tracking a marker with ↑↓, typed mental arithmetic, and pressing Space when one
of three gauges (FUEL / EGT / HYD) goes red. Live percentage per task plus an
OVERALL figure updating continuously. **Two attempts, framed explicitly as
"improvement between attempts measures learning ability."**

Their trainer carries 11 modules — mental arithmetic, number series, numerical
reasoning, compass bearing, FAST multitasking, short-term memory, spatial
orientation from instruments, slalom tracking, two-axis coordination, a
flight-director control task, and a 60-item Big Five questionnaire — plus a
paywalled ILS multitask. Free, no sign-up, monetised through a €49.90 interview
bank.

### 2.2 PilotSphere (India, ₹1,249) — the direct competitor

Modules: Ball & Rudder, Multitasking, Maths (30-min, unique set per attempt),
Physics, Cognitive Reasoning, and **an APQ with 161 statements** producing radar
profiles, a profile code, locus of control and safety-culture readouts — free to
all members. Free trials: 3 attempts on psychomotor, 2 on knowledge. The
simulator itself sits behind sign-in, so I did not run it.

Their two marketed differentiators: **a fresh scenario every attempt** (aimed at
PilotAssessments, who reuse one paper) and **rudder-pedal support** alongside
joystick and keyboard. Their legal disclaimer is a good model — explicit
non-affiliation, explicit statement that no item is sourced from Symbiotics.

## 3. Ghost Aviator's sim, measured against that

Live at `/adapt-test`, in the sitemap, crawlable, seven modules. Read in full:
`lib/adapt/*` and `app/adapt-test/*`.

### 3.1 Where you are already ahead

These are not consolation prizes — they are things neither competitor does.

- **Free, no account, unlimited.** PilotSphere charges ₹1,249; Symbiotics £68.
- **Seeded generation *plus* exact re-sit.** PilotSphere sells "fresh every time"
  as its edge over "same paper every time". You have both: fresh papers by
  default, and "sit this exact paper again" from the seed. **You are not
  marketing this and you should be.**
- **The scoring rule is published.** The criterion ladder is printed on the
  result page. Nobody else shows their cuts.
- **You refuse to invent norms.** Criterion-based until n≥500, then a *frozen,
  versioned* snapshot — not a rolling mean that would silently deflate identical
  performances as practice lifts the population. That is a real psychometric
  argument, correctly made.
- **The tracking score is better than the industry's.** Scoring as *percentage
  of the disturbance actually cancelled*, against a computed do-nothing baseline,
  is meaningful without any reference population. A raw RMSE is not.
- **Fixed 50 Hz sampling decoupled from frame rate**, and input-class separation
  so a phone is never pooled with a joystick. Neither competitor claims either.
- **Divided attention penalises fixation** via the spread between streams. Flying
  three at 70% beats one at 100% and two at 40% — that is the correct lesson and
  I did not see it anywhere else.
- **The party-line radio stream.** Acknowledge your callsign, ignore other
  traffic. Genuinely unique, and genuinely a flight-deck skill.
- **Every wrong option explains its own specific mistake.**

### 3.2 The gaps, ranked by value ÷ cost

#### TIER 1 — do these first

**(1) Correct the provisional numbers. Three are now known first-party.**
`session.mjs` carries a comment saying the counts and clocks are provisional and
isolated in one table so they can be fixed the moment real figures arrive. They
have arrived:

| Module | You have | Real analogue | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aviation Maths | 20 items / 30 min | Maths Progressive **20 / 30** | ✅ already correct |
| Spatial & Pattern | 36 / 30 min | Cognitive Reasoning **36 / 30** | ✅ already correct |
| Physics & Mechanical | 20 / **25 min** | Physics Progressive 20 / **30 min** | **change to 30 min** |
| Control & Co-ordination | **60 sec** | Ball Game **~5 min** | **lengthen substantially** |
| Divided Attention | **180 sec** | FAST **~15 min** | **lengthen, in phases** |

The 60-second tracking run is the sharpest mismatch. A five-minute ball game is
a different task from a one-minute one — fatigue and drift-recovery only appear
after the first minute or two, and that is much of what is being measured.

**(2) Score the learning rate.** This is the single biggest missing idea. The
real FAST is run twice *specifically* so improvement can be read as
trainability; the Ball Game reports improvement against a comparison group. You
already store attempt history and compute a trend — the missing piece is the
*paired-attempt structure*: offer "Attempt 1 / Attempt 2" back to back on the
same seed family, and report a learning slope beside the stanine. Low cost, and
it teaches the student the thing the real battery is actually looking for.

**(3) Make Divided Attention escalate.** Yours runs three minutes at constant
load. The real one gets harder as it goes — more streams, tighter windows. Three
phases inside one run, with per-phase scores, converts a good module into a
faithful one and makes the collapse point visible, which is the coachable moment.

#### TIER 2 — real gaps, moderate work

**(4) Ramp difficulty inside the knowledge papers.** "Progressive" is in the real
product's name. Your generators produce uniform difficulty. Tag each family with
a tier and have `buildPaper` ramp Foundation → Intermediate → Advanced. It also
makes the per-family report far more useful: "you hold up to Intermediate and
fall over on Advanced ratios" is actionable; "68%" is not.

**(5) Fill the two missing Cognitive Reasoning sections.** The real test's four
sections are numerical, verbal, spatial/relationship, **working memory**. Your
36/30 module covers spatial + number series + clock angles. Missing: **working
memory** (a panel of instrument readouts flashes, then blanks, reproduce the
values — both competitors ship this) and **verbal reasoning** (passage plus
true/false/cannot-say). Note your English Language module maps to Symbiotics'
separate *English Language* test, not to the verbal-reasoning section — they are
different instruments and you currently have neither of the two missing sections.

**(6) Report sub-scores per module.** You compute `byFamily` internally and throw
it away at the headline. The real Cognitive Reasoning report gives an overall
score plus one per section. Surface what you already have.

**(7) Add the mechanical-reasoning strand.** Mechanical Reasoning is a separate
instrument in the real catalogue — pulleys, gears, levers, linkages. Your physics
bank touches it once (moment balance). Either grow that strand inside the physics
module or split it out.

#### TIER 3 — worth doing, lower urgency

**(8) Show the colour band next to the stanine.** The real reports are
colour-banded, and the Ball Game specifically uses **sten (1–10)**, not stanine.
Your stanine choice is defensible and published — keep it as the headline — but
add the band, because familiarisation with the report a student will actually
receive *is the product*.

**(9) Extend the attitudes module — for format shock, not for psychometrics.**
Six scenarios versus 275+ in the real APQ (PilotSphere ships 161). Your
weight-zero decision is right and should not change. But the value here is
teaching a student what an untimed hour of forced-choice most/least-like-me
actually feels like, so they do not meet it cold. Thirty to forty items would do
that; six cannot.

**(10) Add English listening.** The real English Language test is "English
Language, **Listening** and Comprehension". Yours is text-only — but you already
play audio in the divided-attention radio stream, so the capability exists.

**(11) Add a rudder/yaw axis to the ball game.** The real one is a ball on a
plate responding to directional changes; PilotSphere sells rudder support as its
differentiator. A third axis on gamepad axis 2, or A/D keys, would cover the
harder variant. Keep input-class separation so it never pools with 2-axis runs.

**(12) A keepable report.** The real product ends in a downloadable PDF. Iron
Rule 3 forbids download affordances on site content — **this is a genuine
conflict and your call, not mine.** A screen-only "report card" view is the
middle path if you want one.

### 3.3 One thing to be careful about

Your page and its FAQ schema say the modules mirror airline screening. They do,
and the disclaimers are honest — but PilotSphere's legal block is stronger than
yours: explicit non-affiliation with Symbiotics, explicit statement that no item
is sourced from their content, and an explicit "refer to Symbiotics directly for
accurate information about the official test". **Adopt that shape.** You are
using their trademark descriptively, which is legitimate, and saying so plainly
costs nothing and forecloses an argument.

## 3.4 What was BUILT on 2026-08-10, after this audit

Capt. Pahil's instruction on reading the above: make it look and behave like the real
thing, ask students for a free sign-up, and record results so the data can improve the
simulator. All of the following is done, tested and lint-clean.

**The clocks are corrected.** Physics 25 → **30 min**. Tracking 60 sec → **5 min**.
Multitasking 3 flat min → **15 min in three escalating phases**. Maths and Spatial were
already right. The whole battery now runs about two and a quarter hours, and the brief
screen derives that figure from the registry rather than restating it.

**The multitasking run escalates**, as the real one does — events arrive closer together
AND the response window tightens, phase by phase. The gauge deliberately does not speed
up: it is the background task, and the skill is holding a background task while the
foreground gets louder. The result names the phase where performance stepped down.

**The knowledge papers are progressive.** Every family in all four banks carries a
declared Foundation / Intermediate / Advanced tier, and a paper deals them in that order.
The report shows accuracy per tier and names the student's ceiling, which is the useful
thing a flat paper cannot tell them.

**The report is shaped like the real one** — the stanine stays as the headline, with the
**sten 1-10 score, a colour band, and a ten-box scale bar** beside it, plus sub-scores per
section. The band boundaries are ours and the page says so.

**Learning rate is scored** (`lib/adapt/learning.mjs`) — least-squares slope across
sittings, so one bad night cannot flip a trend, with a ceiling-effect flag and a hard
refusal to draw a trend through fewer than three points.

**The tracking run reports minute by minute**, each minute against the do-nothing
baseline for *that* minute, and classifies the run as held / faded / built.

**Free sign-up is asked for, never required.** Every module still runs signed-out. Signed
in, results save to `adapt_results` (RLS: own rows only) with the score AND the breakdown —
per-tier, per-family, per-phase, per-minute — which is what makes the data usable for
fixing the simulator. The dashboard shows the student's sittings and learning curve.
**Still refused, signed in or out: the questions, the answers, and anything from the
attitudes questionnaire beyond the fact that it was finished.** Enforced by
`results-core.test.mjs`, not by care.

**Two real defects were found on the way**, both pre-existing and neither visible in
review: a distractor collision in the English briefing item that could throw and kill a
student's whole paper, and a wrong object path in the results builder that would have
saved every multitasking row with all streams null.

**The deploy-order window is handled.** The site ships from a git push and the table is
created by hand in Supabase, so whichever happens second leaves a gap. In that gap a
signed-in student used to get an apology quoting a Postgres schema-cache message. A missing
table is now recognised (`42P01` / `PGRST205`) and reported as "saving is not switched on
quite yet" — no database text, no implication the result was lost. Proved against a server
returning exactly what PostgREST returns: `npm run verify:adapt-notready`. This means the
code and the migration can be applied in either order.

**Still open from this audit:** working-memory and verbal-reasoning sections, the
mechanical-reasoning strand, English listening, a rudder axis, the longer questionnaire,
and the keepable-report / Iron-Rule-3 question. And buying the £68 bundle.

**Three findings that change what we thought:**

1. **English IS part of the cadet battery.** Our §1.2 treated the English Language test

   products. A real cadet report contains an English section. **Our English module is
   therefore not "ours rather than theirs" — it belongs.** Correcting the earlier claim.
2. **"Hazardous attitudes" are explicitly part of what airlines read.** His description
   says the report covers "what hazardous attitudes airlines look for". Our
   attitudes-and-airmanship module, built on the FAA hazardous-attitude inventory and

3. **The personality answers generate the interview.** He states that the questionnaire
   responses "automatically generate tough questions for your final interview panel."

**Note the report is dominated by the personality half** — roughly 9 of his 20 minutes,
against ~2 minutes for all three knowledge tests combined. We have been building the
knowledge modules hardest and treating the questionnaire as a side feature. The real

### 6.4 What this suggests building next

Ranked by how much it differentiates us and how little it costs:

1. ~~**Show a panel-question preview after the attitudes questionnaire.**~~ **BUILT
   2026-08-12** — `lib/adapt/panel-questions.mjs`, rendered by `PanelQuestions` on the
   result page. Driven by the attitudes the student actually showed, breadth-first, capped
   at five, with a consistency prompt when the paired answers disagree. The questions are
   ORIGINAL, written against the published FAA hazardous-attitude model the questionnaire
   is already scored on — the preamble states plainly they are not the real test's items,
   and a test fails if that sentence is ever softened. Each carries a `listeningFor` line,
   which is the half that actually coaches and the half no free resource in India offers.
2. **Restructure the attitudes report into the real report's sections** — Role, Personal
   Style, Character, Work Environment, Key Attributes. Familiarisation is the product.
3. **A "silhouette"-style visual profile.** We do not know the real one's construction and
   must not fake it — but a shape-based profile summary is the format candidates meet.
4. **Extend the questionnaire.** Six scenarios cannot carry the weight the real report
   gives this half. This is now evidence-backed, not just a hunch from the item count.
